## **The Empty Window of the City**

Once again, I really like Elaine’s understanding of the texture of objects and images. Against the background of night blue, most of the works on display this time are run through flickering in the city, and even copied dummy words used for design and layout. Elaine responded to the topic raised by the curator - "The Empty Period" with a bunch of "meaningless" objects. Kazaf, one of the curators, feels that window period is a good personal thing. If one person does not open his own window, it will be difficult for others to enter another person's window. I believe that for many city dwellers, the "meaningless" things Elaine found in the city are, first of all, the only windows through which we can stare blankly and even project our emotions. These flashes of light that appear mistakenly or randomly in the face of the city that is constantly moving forward are sometimes failures, and they are also existences beyond what the city can plan. I remembered that in the "Late Night Minibus Ride" series, Zheng Hong also said that he felt that these occasional lights were like stars, which were mysterious and reminded him of the relationship between man and nature. Elaine, on the other hand, uses the folders that city dwellers are best at using, and archives these pockets in the city's crevices, like black holes, for later use. It is assumed that the next and every emotion can be contained in the same container, and the emotions are processed like a factory.

Staring at the flash of the city at night, or just like reading Xixi's out-of-focus poems and copying meaningless words under the street lights with labor gloves: overhead is the broken street lamp, sitting on an old wooden chair from nowhere, and under the feet is the carpet that has been accumulated for many years in the house. Exhibition:

Fantasy
Artist Artist :

Lewis LAU

Venue:
Art Projects Gallery

Date Date :

2021.09.11

The first association with Liu Yantao's "Dreamland" must be Zhong Menghong's "Sunshine".

The works exhibited in "Illusion" all reflect Zuo Liu Yantao's sensitivity to light and shadow. Almost all works deliberately distinguish between light and dark positions, front and rear backgrounds and mid-focus and out-of-focus positions. The light and mid-focus positions are very realistic, while other positions are treated with a technique similar to impressionism. These positions, especially the shadows on the ground, have become places where Zuo's artist invests a lot of emotion. There is the abyss under the railroad in "The Three Musketeers", there is also the surge of "Facade" and the sea of ​​blood in "Nuances". Sunlight makes things dazzlingly clear. Only under the shadows is there room for emotions, but it is extremely dangerous and one could drown at any time.

"Facade" is called "Facade" in English, which is the outer garment of architecture and city. In French, it is "Frontage" or "Face". Many of the works depict places that are inconspicuous in the city and do not have "Face". These places are scattered, depressed, disordered, withered trees growing and buildings failing. The people in The Three Musketeers, Nuances and The Solitude are lonely in these spaces, and only the only small painting in the exhibition, Joshi Kōsei, shows a pair of high school girls who are youthful, cheerful, and energetic, but the place they are in is unclear. Lewis's paintings, from size, material to painting method, embody the emotions of the absurd, the only space to be in, and the form of their habitat.
