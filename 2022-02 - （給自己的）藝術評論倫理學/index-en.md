The world has come to a standstill, and my body has also come to a standstill for a while. This time I would like to share what I have been thinking about recently. When viewing the exhibition, I was asked from time to time whether I was creating art. Regardless of whether I was or not, I knew very well that I did not aspire to be an artist, and I have enjoyed the latter position more since I was a child. But I often feel strange about this, especially when we talk about art. Without art, there is no art discussion. Artists are the ones who face the world honestly with their bodies. Besides, there are many things that do not require interpretation at all and cannot be back-translated at all. Sometimes, I even think this kind of "secondary creation" is a bit unethical.

Since the opening of the account, most of the followers here (seem to be) are from the art circle (thank you), which is a good exercise for me. Then, I began to wonder how artists understand the existence of art criticism (to be honest, I don’t think I am an art critic yet, but regardless, I will use this word for now), and began to think about the ethics of criticism. Coincidentally, I finally had time to catch up on Issues 5 and 6 of Curatography recently, and came across "Ethics of Curation", which I thought would be useful to discuss.

## **Interpretation**

Roughly speaking, the harmony between curation and art criticism is that both of them understand and then present the artwork. "Curatorial Ethics" believes that the issue of "speaking for others" is the core of curatorial ethics. The article borrows anthropological discussions and examples of Taiwanese aboriginal art planning to point out the opposition between subject and object, and even doubts whether this action of "speaking for others" is possible (Chen Mengxi, 2021). This is exactly the question I often think about when doing art criticism - am I saying the right thing?

I believe that we no longer need to discuss the issue of who has the right to interpret. I also believe that all works of art have been commented on, and artists must have faced this experience. But for curators and art critics, the problem is that these interpretations are public, and this involves the ethics of creating public knowledge, which may also affect the audience's perspective on artworks and artists. The former is easy to understand. It refers to issues such as right or wrong interpretation, right or wrong viewpoint, etc. Art critics need to be responsible to the public and to "truth". The latter seems to be more difficult to grasp, and there is no clear specification, because it is related to the original intention of the creator, the space they want to leave for readers, and the "image" (if any) they want to present, etc. Furthermore, if it is impossible to "speak for others" - using the subject to understand and reproduce the object, it is even more difficult to master the above.

## **Power**

Although both are interpretations, the most discordant thing between curators and critics is the construction of power - curators often have a certain degree of consensus with artists, and both parties have the power to jointly set the presentation method for works and exhibitions. However, for art critics, this is not inevitable. More often than not, art critics are just uninvited guests.

"Curatorial Ethics" borrows Derrida's interpretation of "Hospitality" and applies it to the subject-guest relationship in curating - the article believes that whether the inviter is hospitable depends on whether the inviter can include the guest in the invitation, and then sublimate it, treating the guest as the "host" of the party, entertaining him openly and unconditionally (Chen Mengxi, 2021). Regardless of whether this is a good curatorial approach, we can imagine that the art critic is very likely not even in this power relationship. Even as the "guest" of the exhibition, unlike the curatorial relationship, the subject-guest relationship can change positions so easily. This not only makes the interpretive power of art critics more questionable than curators, but also makes their ethics more uncertain.

## **Personal Cultivation**

Another thing that I think can be used for reference in "Curatorial Ethics" is the article's discussion of institutional curators and independent curators. curator) - The article believes that institutional curators have more information to understand their curatorial ethics than independent curators. At least in Hopewell, there are certain explicit provisions here to define responsibilities and work content (of course we cannot believe that these are the rules of curatorial ethics). However, on the contrary, independent curatorial projects often vary, and the work content can also vary depending on the person and personality, which is a universal code of ethics. In the final analysis, the morality that independent curators have to face is most likely (or only) personal morality - how to professionalize one's own morality in facing the world, put it into action, and put it into curation (Chen Mengxi, 2021).

A person who makes art criticism on behalf of an institution is most likely already a curator. When talking about art critics, almost all of them can be said to be independent (at least I am), so the moral paths of art critics and independent curators may be similar. To be honest, this conclusion makes me relieved. At least for me, it is reasonable and lucky to put daily ethics in my "professional" (some professions do not mix personal and professional).

## **Conclusion (for myself)**

"Curatorial Ethics" temporarily concludes the discussion of curatorial ethics with peace of mind, but this is an article written for myself, so I will not conclude my own thinking here. To be honest, in fact, "Useless Stone" ultimately hopes to be exposed to a wider public, but I feel that I have not yet reached the end of the spectrum, and I have not yet decided how to do it. For the time being, I will allow it to continue to be exposed to the public in the form of personal notes. For me, I may not be a good creator, but I also hope to be a better audience and a better person!
