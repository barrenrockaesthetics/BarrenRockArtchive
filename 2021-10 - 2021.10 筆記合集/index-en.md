## **Utoki**

I have always felt that what photography needs to look at is never the form itself, but everything behind the form and how it is created. For landscape photography, time is especially important.

For Tang Jieyi, Mao Shi is the moment between dark night and light, it is hope and the beginning of a new day. In the picture, the photographer appears at times that most people would not be around, recording the light, shadow and angles that only belong to each period, creating a sense of distance from life. These temporally unique images, coupled with the location chosen by the artist, refer to the political meaning of representational space. At the same time, these lights and shadows also echo the experience of waking up in a discordant place in the city two summers ago. It's calm at night, but some early mornings are not. 𠴱Shi may not have time to appreciate it properly, but to establish a relationship with the rare magical moment in the city, I revisited the places in "Mao Shi" many times. They are very beautiful, and the unique and unfamiliar images make memories and emotions secretly emerge from time to time.

Exhibition:

Moiré Artists Artists: Silvester Mok

Venue: Yrellag Gallery

Date Date :

2021.10.14

Ceramic is warm here, is touch. In "Moiré", the work presents the "author" as an indispensable role in determining shape, color and everything else about the work, and so the 3D printer cannot help us decide all of this - the drooping mud paddle across the hole, the wrinkles that are unbalanced due to mechanics, etc. The "author" exists in imagination, design, temperature here and materials. The work is slightly warm and gentle, and although they are folded, the shape and color radiate a soft and comfortable feeling that makes you want to touch it!

The difference between tools and manpower is furthermore, there is plenty of space between them for the "author" to intervene. If ceramics is the relationship between temperature and materials, then "Moiré" is the desire and imagination to get in touch with all this, just adding a 3D printer in the middle. Extending earlier thinking about cement and cities, it seems that even if we don’t physically understand the world, we still have the space and ways to remain curious and relate to them.

Exhibition:

Dropped Artist Artist :

Louis Max Nixon

Venue: Osage Hong Kong

Date Date :

2021.10.16

As soon as you enter the museum, you can see the artist's research results on stones, gravity, and materials. The artist's research methods are similar to scientific research - fixed factors (iron platform, gravity) plus variable factors (stones). The presentation method of "Dropped" shows the entire platform, which is worse than a stone. The audience can see the entire impact process, force, center of gravity, weight... It is incompatible with the numbers and measurements that "science" is generally concerned about. In addition, the quantity, volume and texture on display also make the whole thing shocking.

Newton’s Third Law says that with action force, there will be reaction force; the reaction force relative to gravity in “Dropped” (even) is the force that space junk can go into space, which is also the focus of the artist’s research - how and why did space junk go into space? The exhibition summarizes the footage of silly watermelon astronauts "dropping garbage", and 3D print production of space garbage archive for research (how did you expect there will be Watch's wet wipes, throw him, better than Lao Si disinfection better!). In addition, the artist also simulated footage of these space garbage floating in space, coupled with pop songs, to contrast the absurdity of humans dropping garbage in space. Contrast with the gravity of the previous part, one heavy and one light, space "fall" rare earths than us, humans responded with garbage, "Dropped" presents a whole asymmetry.

In fact, I have been thinking about space garbage lately, because I was thinking, isn't supernova the most sustainable incinerator? Why don't we drop trash and go into space? Dropped states that the reason we care about environmental issues in space is that garbage poses a danger to spaceflight, or that in the end, these concerns are nothing more than the product of anthropocentrism.

The research results towards rocks, gravitational force and materials are displayed openly at the main stage of the exhibition. The research method conducted by artist Louis Max Nixon is somehow close to a scientific one - with fixed factors (the iron table and gravitational force) and a variable factor (the rocks). The display of Dropped showed the whole process of collision, power, centre of force, weight and all other complicated measures as a whole in a single picture. This is how an artistic research may different from a ‘general’ scientific one, from general measures to particular displays, and Dropped sounded an impressive one.

Newton’s Third Law stated - whenever there is action force, there shall be reaction force. For Dropped, the reaction force of the gravitational force is that of bringing space debris from the ground to places up there. This is also believed to be the major research question of the project - how and why are those debris up there? The exhibition collected footage by careless astronauts, re-mode and archived space debris in 3D printed models. The artist also composed a simulation video of space debris flying around with pop music. How ridiculous it could be, when space ‘gave’ us precious metals and we, humans, reply by rocketing debris. Dropped showed the tilted relationship we are with space.

Recently, I have also thought of throwing rubbish into space. I mean, it seems that supernovas could be the most sustainable incinerator we could seize (though practically not possible). But, if that is true, why don’t we rocket our waste out there. The reason why we need to care about space sustainability Dropped gave us, was about the safety of exercising space travel. I don’t know, but I hope that is not a product of anthropocentrism.

Performance:

After Enso,

Sound Form 2021: Present_Present Artist Artist : Kin LAM Kin LAM

Venue: F Hall, Tai Kwun

Date Date :

2021.10.31

There should be no need to say how important "now" is in performance. For Kin Lam, "now" is embodied in "concentrating on every moment", which is also where he needs to spend the most effort after returning to Hong Kong from the United States. Born in percussion, then drilled low-cost, ready-made (Found Object) as an instrument, you can see on the Internet he teaches using a speaker as a synthesizer (Synthesizer) and light tube as an instrument, but not long after returning to Hong Kong he quickly found this place no space for him to do these experiments, vinyl and turntable (Turntable) is his most "practised" sound this year.

Percussion, to put it crudely, is playing with beats, and counting beats is the technique of dividing time. The time on the turntable is measured in "circles". Kin Lam broke the vinyl and reassembled it, adding foreign objects. He deliberately transformed the cracks and foreign objects into "beats" on the "clock" of the turntable, and became "now" in the form of analog data (Analog).

The "now" presented by vinyl comes from Kin Lam's "private group" on the one hand, and "memory" found sounds from all over the world on the other hand, including ambient sounds and recordings of self-reported murders. Carrying the past and becoming the present, apart from the left, there is another way to see how "After Enso" presents the "present" vision. It is probably Kin Lam, and it is also our most "present" topic.

Film:

Memoria Director Director:

Apichatpong Weerasethakul

Year Year : 2021

Date Date :

2021.10.31

Nature, fantasy, light and shadow, perfect composition, etc., etc., there are too many synonyms that represent Apichatpong's movie. "Memoria" is very powerful. After watching it, you will immediately connect with the world and nature!

"Memoria" opens this fantasy-like story with sound at the beginning. In fact, you don't know much about Tilda, the goddess, who is doing things. Everything about his occupation, places he has been to, and people he has met is very vague. However, the story is connected with a large number of themes, constantly shuttled back and forth between occultism, anthropology and psychoanalysis. The most obvious one is the relationship between memory, dreams and nature. The film quotes the culture and symbols of tribal republican dreams, thinking about Jung's discussion of knowledge and cognition in the collective unconscious (Collective Unconscious). Is there a basis for our republican existence in the world to understand ourselves, our nation, history and the future better than we do? The protagonist discusses poetry and sound at the beginning of the film, and by the end of the film, after connecting with others, there may be disagreements.

In addition, science fiction elements that may be considered abrupt also play a very important role. On the one hand, it represents a certain fantasy of human understanding of ancient civilization. At the end of the film, he is also the "rival" of the forces of nature, becoming one of the ways to explain all the above incomprehensions. The film also mentions tripping, regardless of the director's attitude, these psychotropic drugs have different uses and status in the world of ancient civilization, modernity, and even the new age, but the effect should be the same. This once again connects memory, dreams, nature, history, and the future. In addition to so many of the above themes, the film also involves sound, globalization, and even more discussion, but not too much to say here.

Returning to the artistic discussion, the film's handling on the screen is very good. The film's approach to creating a sense of illusion and reality also reminds me of "Picnic on the Road", which is of course much more mature and without long long shots. A large number of real scenes, coupled with only the irrationality in the details of life, has created a very strong sense of strangeness. In terms of space and scene design, the film does not explicitly tell you the location of the story, just using editing to take the protagonist through these nameless spaces, making the audience believe that it all happened reasonably, but never see how they get in and out of the space. This reminds me of what is said in Inception that one of the characteristics of dreaming is that you don’t know why you appear in this space.

Apichatpong, who studied architecture, not only uses the space and contours reflected in a large number of low-light scenes to create atmosphere and stories, he also makes each screen presented with perfect composition. This also allows the film to construct multiple spaces using the film frame and sound on multiple occasions, with characters repeatedly negotiating with “nature” outside the frame and voices entering the story from outside the film frame multiple times to endorse “nature,” or the subconscious. The film frame also appears from time to time with subtle dynamics, understated but harmoniously prompting the story time and background. A series of perfect calculations makes the screen mysterious and shocking!

The amount of information is too huge, so I’ll leave it to everyone to think about it slowly.
