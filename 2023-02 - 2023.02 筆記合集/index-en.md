The song of 611 will always connect you with the grassland and the sky. The place was not big, I sat against the wall, closed my eyes, and I imagined we were all in a truck cabinet where I didn’t know where to go, listening to 611 tell stories about how he wandered outside, how he got together in the fire, listening to others sing, listening to discord. Last year I watched "On the Grassland, We Start Dancing", a documentary about the Taiwanese literary and artistic group "Wild Youth" searching for the grassland in downtown Taipei. Their "120 Grassland Autonomous Region" ended in failure, but the question they asked was still unanswered: Where did the city's grasslands go? Is there any grassland in Hong Kong that can be compared to the song, walking from one horizon to another?

Yan Yan doesn't sing much about his new album this time. The set list is mostly Cantonese songs from the 80s and 90s. This time he also purposely requested that the human voice not fall a lot of reverb as before, just use simple guitar accompaniment and sing with a pure voice. He believes that what song he wants to sing and how he sings it depends on his age. When he said this, I immediately came to mind how Joni Mitchell of the 2022 Newsport Folk Festival reinterpreted Both Sides Now, his 50-year-old piece, after losing his voice after suffering from a brain tumor. Isn't Folk exactly a genre that instills the present, and the emotions accumulated in the past, into the singing voice by understanding its own voice, its own language? ! It is also how Folk is related to the individual, to the place, and to the nation. Yan Ren's decision to reinterpret the old song was a choice after time. The singing of "One Time", "When" and "Choice" will naturally resonate with us who live in the same place and experience the same days. When he sang "Difficult to be the boundary between good and evil", his choice of singing (lowering or not), choosing to sing Ye Zhentang or Mai Zhicheng's voice (good or evil), all chose what he is now, how to see this city. The same goes for 611 and Wilson Tsang's singing, which are voices chosen after understanding themselves and being sculpted by the days.

I remember in college class once heard a saying is: one of the differences between Hong Kong pre-millennial pop songs is that the former's melody is mostly simpler less words, there is more space between sounds, can than singers put emotions between sounds, some way here is the singer has more space to choose the way of singing Whether this distinction is accurate or not will not be discussed here, but it seems to be a pretty good entry point to explain why Cantonese songs from the 1980s and 1990s were chosen by Yan Ren to appear in Lai Chi Kok and in "He Guang and Dust". If the urban grassland is no longer there, then the landscape of Hong Kong is still giving birth to some. How can it belong to the melody of Hong Kong? When one day we are all in a different place, one day we are lucky enough to be together in front of the fire, what kind of song will we sing to tell our story?
