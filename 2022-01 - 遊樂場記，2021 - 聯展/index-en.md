In the past two years, urban space has been lost, and it has become even worse after the epidemic, especially play space, which is a big challenge for children and parents. The importance of reviewing the play space during the epidemic is to understand how we grew up under difficulties. The Intelligent Children's Play Association and the Expansion of Public Spaces earlier released the results of their research on children's games during the epidemic, hoping to re-emphasize that play spaces should be a city's infrastructure.

// Man is only fully human when he plays! // (Schiller, F., & Snell, R., 2004).

## **Play in "non-space"! **

Antiplayground and Yi, located in the busy city, regard play as a necessity of life. With no space left, they explore "non-space" to show their determination that they must "play". In "Speech/Speech Philosophy", the team bases the issue on the contradiction between "games" and "framework" - games are about imagination, about open thinking, about actions that leave the frame and invest in the future. "Speech/Lecture Philosophy" begins with Uncle Yan (Good Youth Poison Room) proposing a theoretical framework on the importance of games. Performance (as an artistic method) intervenes in the middle, breaking the theoretical framework on the open framework with toy sound effects and mixing, adding rubber balls, and gradually becoming an invitation to play, opening up the power relationship between the speaker, artist and audience.

On the one hand, antiplayground raised the importance of play, and on the other hand, they also cooperated with Cong Quartet to show how they imagine "non-space" play modes. The performance is introduced with the sound effect of a rubber ball, and then enters the quartet. The limited repetition of phrases is displayed in the form of discord - the alternation of voices, collision and mixing, all constructing and destroying the space created by music, constantly defining and exploring the composition process of "non-space". Finally, after the invitation from Gumball, the musician left resolutely. The sound is abstract, but the melody is concrete. The audience has enough material to visualize the amusement park in their minds at will, and also has the right to destroy it at will. This kind of space that is within the mind, can come and go by itself, but is in harmony with others in time and space. I believe it is the "non-playground" that antiplayground believes we need.

antiplayground proves that our ability to play has not expired and is enough to resist the "disappearance of playgrounds". We just lost motivation at a certain time. antiplayground uses reason to wake us up after we have been baptized by the social frame. Coincidentally, "Our Abstract Game Landscape" on display in Heqiu researches the other side of time and re-understands the places that appeared before we "became gears" and influenced our play habits.

## **Abstract and Curiosity**

The planner Helen relied on curiosity about an old photo of the Shek Lei Amusement Park to look back to the days when the amusement park had not "disappeared". "Our abstract playscape" is the result of this "curiosity". Before Hong Kong's playgrounds were occupied by formulaic components, Shek Lei used to be such a beautiful sculptural playground. This was a game language that artist Isamu Noguchi had to work hard to achieve in both China and the West. Shek Lei Playground can be said to be an important legacy in the development history of Hong Kong's playgrounds. The plan starts from Shek Lei Playground and extends to more excellent but neglected playgrounds. In addition to displaying Shek Lei Playground and several other abstract playscapes, the exhibition also lays out many important timelines and famous abstract landforms in the history of the world's playgrounds. Discerning viewers can discover the dialogue between them, as well as the subtle relationship between Hong Kong's playgrounds and the international stage, and appreciate the avant-garde attitude our playgrounds have faced the world.

The documentary of Stone Hedges Playground is another treasure in the exhibition. The footage is believed to have been filmed by the designer Paul Selinger himself and was discovered and made public many years later due to Helen’s research. In addition to recording the skillful hands of Hong Kong's construction industry at that time, the clip is even more fascinating is the children who were already playing in the playground before it was completed, which perfectly embodies free play (Free Play) and creativity. Games are children’s daily life and are an important way for them to understand the world and learn to relate to others. Free play is highly valued by early childhood education theories such as Montessori and Waldorf. Its focus is to provide unlimited possible games and cultivate children’s creativity. The playground is a place created for games, and abstract language allows players to interpret and create on their own. When discussing abstract game landscapes, in addition to reflecting on the existing free landscape, we are also concerned about the thinking patterns we hope to have (or keep).

## **Play as Rights and Practices**

Some people would say that games are "undeveloped art production" (Luostarinen, N., & Hautio, M., 2019). Their compatibility includes the ability to express freedom and creativity. The artistic language of antiplayground and the research mission of "Hong Kong Abstract Play Landscape" are obviously different. The two can echo each other, not only because they have a common medium - playground, but also because play and art practice share a common concern with creative practice and the ability to create spaces and things. antiplayground reminds us that we can create our own space in troubled times, and this ability needs to be cultivated in a free playground; creativity is what we should have, and enjoying a free playground is our basic right.

P.S. If you are curious about how to design games, game designer Cas Holman talked about her experience in designing free games in Netflix's Abstract: The Art of Design. Recently, M+ also used the Imagination Playground she designed to hold workshops for parents and children. You may want to pay attention to the Intelligent Children's Play Association. They hold regular courses to teach techniques for creating free play.
