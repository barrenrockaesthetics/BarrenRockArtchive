# 2022.06 Notes Collection

Exhibition:

Respinning East Asia Series 2: Where to weave and where to unwrap the web

Work: Nothingness and Countryside

Artist Artist :

Li Jizhong

Yugo Isaji

Venue: Factory 6 CHAT

Date Date :

2022.06.05

In 1936, the Japanese Kwantung Army formulated a peasant immigration plan and forced citizens to move to Manchukuo. Less than ten years later, Japan was defeated, and those who immigrated to Manchuria had to be forced to move back to Japan again. They had to become second-class citizens, lose all identities, and stay in the void between national borders.

The work reshapes the emotions of 7 days with seven videos: it starts with farmers disappointed with the geology of their land and longing for other places, stepping into the endless forest, even if they need to abandon their daughter and mother, they can only move forward. When they arrived, they found that the coins were not accessible, and they could not settle down, and had nowhere to stay. 7 videos are shown in 7 channels, played day by day in 7 consecutive screens, guiding (forcing) the viewer to move forward in the space, with only forward time, to the next day. Even if you haven’t digested yesterday’s toil, parting, and pain, you must move forward. Whenever you walk through the day, unprocessed emotions will keep piling up, and your daughter's voice - "お母さん, お母さん" - will keep coming from the previous day's footage from behind to your brain, asking you: "Where is this place? How long is it?", with no room for rest and rejection. The migrants ultimately fail to settle down and can only sit stunned at the border, where they suddenly and in a timely manner face the camera and ask the audience on the other side of the video the definition of home. The work finally pulls the audience back to the entrance of the journey with sound effects, but suddenly in the darkness, ending on the final screen, playing the audience to the middle point of the entrance and exit, leaving them in a dilemma.

For Li Jizhong, he returns to the archives in a consistent way and continues to uncover the forgotten emotions in history; for the exhibition, it is a discrete story under the great power game, and it is a piece of hair that was lost between weaving and dismantling and no one picked it up. These pieces of hair are loaded with a lot of emotions, scattered in the big narrative.
