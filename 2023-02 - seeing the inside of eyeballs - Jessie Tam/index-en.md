## **// From the moment I met it, I knew it missed the old house, because the blue water marks on the surface were evidence that it was crying //**

Jessie’s personal plan for returning to Hong Kong for the first time - "It Has to Be ApartIt Has to Be" "Alone" (hereinafter referred to as "AA"), starting from the plaster fragments she encountered during her wanderings that looked like eyeballs, and paired with the exhibition of a series of discarded daily objects, she imagined a story about preparing to return to her old home for the plaster eyeballs.

The British and Hong Kong small coin rubbings I came across soon after I started. It was based on the fact that after Jessie went to the Netherlands to study, she found two coins that had secretly followed her. With such a beginning, the setting of the abandoned house, and the performances and monologues of Jessie and Eyeball Maniac, it is not difficult for the audience to find that this is a story about the past, leaving home and returning home. In another part of the project - Struggling Artists, Special Edition Jan 2023 (exhibition booklet), there is also an article written by Jessie and art writer MeMe in a discordant style and angle, explaining the "home" in the exhibition. The design of the booklet is also very beautiful, and it is highly recommended that everyone collects a copy (please contact Jessie if you are interested). So I thought, let's look at the rest of the exhibition.

![image](./images/image_2.jpg)

## **// If crying comes from the eyes, I think I have already seen the inside of the eyes //**

Jessie built the exhibition space into the half-open old residence of the eyeball, allowing ideal windows, day, stars and night to live in it. Old Home and Time are the inside of the eyeball, inviting the viewer to enter the light blue "tear" mirror. Surrounded by blue and transparent objects in a cold-colored room, film, acrylic sheets and film can exactly reproduce the feeling of eyes filled with tears and vision being enveloped by water molecules with very light blue color, accompanied by irregular refraction and wrinkles of water droplets. Jessie tells the story of the plaster eyeball as Eyeball Maniac. The three of them’s visions are connected in the performance, leading the audience to see the work and their old home.

After the performance, I asked Jessie if she saw anything in her plaster eyes, and she said it was only pitch black; indeed, the color of the pupils has already told us that the world inside the eyes is black.

## **// I want the eyeballs see themselves cut in half //**

"Half" is another important motif of the exhibition, half day and half night; half virtual and half real; half light and half shadow, all objects are looking for their other half. Jessie said that she always felt that her eyes would become larger and smaller after crying, and in the round mirror, the eyeballs were divided into two halves, with one half inside the eye, looking at the other half - the self that can only be seen when looking in the mirror.

Jessie’s creative media are crazy, including paintings, videos, ready-made objects, installations, texts, performances, role plays, and even diaries, which are mixed with a lot of personal experiences, memories and secrets. In addition to the whisper of small coins in "AA", the moon story told to her eyes in the monologue also comes from her memories of a time at sea, and the identity of the cleaner comes from her experience of working in a Chinese restaurant out of town. Jessie made it clear that she hopes to share her secret conversations with everyday objects through "AA" and tell these secrets to the audience.

Perhaps most of Jessie's works also originated from whispering: In "Be Friend OK?" "(2019)" and "MeMe Theater" playing various stakeholders in the art world in "Will You Pick Me Up Tomorrow" (2021), both express Jessie's current thoughts and emotions in spoken language. MeMe, the character who appears in "Will You Pick Me Up Tomorrow", once again interviews the performer Jessie, who performs a monologue for the exhibition's protagonist, Eyeball Maniac, in the "AA" exhibition booklet. How does she present a world that she imagines/sees due to the found objects she encounters by chance. Jessie has long been playing this game of constantly establishing a third body for herself, then watching herself, and even establishing her own artistic discourse for herself.

## **// I did not ask you to come by, why did you? //**

As Jessie herself mentioned, she is used to writing down concepts and accumulating them into creations bit by bit. Most of these concepts are saved and rolled back in the form of short sentences. If you go back to Jessie's [@artwork_inventory_jessie_tam_](https://www.instagram.com/artwork_inventory_jessie_tam_/) you can find how she has accumulated and reproduced these motifs and imagination in every creation since 2019. They became the names of Household Fragments in "AA", became the monologues of Eyeball Maniac, and were constantly reorganized in the discord text. They quietly appeared next to Jessie like small coins. Jessie used these stars and moons to align and fasten the floor tiles, but she regarded them as dust and sent them away with her own hands, leaving space for the dust to come back again. When you open the exhibition booklet, you will also find that they are following them quietly, flashing back in your mind when you are not paying attention.

## **// to settle down, they cut themselves in half //**

"AA" didn't tell us Eyeball's reaction and whereabouts after seeing her old home. We only know that Eyeball Maniac knew that she would be very sad. Eyeball Maniac finally hangs the spiky moon on the curtain next to the window (projection), telling a story about chasing the moonlight for the eyes. "AA" is not only about the uneasiness about the old home, but also summarizes Jessie's experiences, secret words and art practice outside the home. Half and half of the diary, the exhibition can also be seen as another record of Jessie’s past years besides writing.
